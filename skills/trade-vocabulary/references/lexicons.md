# Sub-sector lexicons

Curated **metric/lever** vocabulary per sub-sector — the words operators manage by.
Each entry: `term — what it means — the customer-visible tell that lets you use it honestly`.

Only terms confirmed correct belong here. A term you're unsure of fails test 2 in the
SKILL ("Fits?") — leave it out and write plain until you've confirmed it.

---

## full_service_restaurant / bar & grill  *(validated in an A/B of report copy, 2026)*

- **beverage attach rate** — share of orders that add a drink; the core bar-program upsell lever — *tell: guests name food but never a drink; flat beverage mix*
- **PPA / check average** — per-person average spend; what an upsell or LTO moves — *tell: low ticket relative to menu price*
- **ticket times** — minutes from order fired to food delivered; "the rail" is where tickets queue, "the pass/window" is where finished plates wait for a runner — *tell: "90 minutes for food," "ticket was lost"*
- **table touch / check-back** — the server returning to a seated table; the service cadence — *tell: "waiter never told us anything," ignored table*
- **FOH / BOH** — front of house (service + bar) vs back of house (kitchen); locates whether the breakdown is service or food — *tell: negatives cluster on service while food earns raves = FOH problem*
- **covers / turns** — covers = guests served; turns = times a table is reseated per service; volume/throughput — *tell: capacity or wait-for-a-table complaints*
- **comp / void** — comping or voiding an item; the service-recovery tool on a blown ticket — *tell: bad experience with no recovery, "manager never came"*
- **LTO** — limited-time offer; a menu item run for a fixed window to drive urgency and price — *tell: fan demand for an item you run rarely*
- **rebook / regulars** — repeat guests; the base quiet service failures bleed — *tell: churn language, "used to love this place"*

Avoid as *slang* (do not use for flavor): "in the weeds," "slammed," "86'd" (the last only when a review literally reports an item was out).

## nail_salon / hair_salon  *(KPI terms only — confirm tells before leaning hard)*

- **rebook rate** — share of clients who book their next appointment before leaving — *tell: reviews about not being able to get back in, or one-time visits*
- **retail attach** — product sold per service visit — *tell: no mention of product, or "they never recommend anything"*
- **column / double-book** — a stylist's booked time; double-booking overlaps two clients — *tell: "waited even with an appointment," rushed service*
- **walk-in gap** — unbooked chair time — *tell: inconsistent availability*
- **chair / booth rental** — how many operators pay to rent a station (business-model lever, not a customer tell)

## hvac / plumbing  *(KPI terms only)*

- **callback** — a return trip for the same problem; the quality/cost killer — *tell: "had to come back," "same issue again"*
- **one-and-done vs maintenance agreement** — one-off calls vs recurring service contracts; the retention lever — *tell: no ongoing relationship mentioned*
- **first-time fix rate** — jobs resolved on the first visit — *tell: repeat-visit complaints*
- **truck stock** — parts carried on the van; drives first-time fix — *tell: "had to order the part / come back"*

---

## cafe / coffee_shop  *(KPI terms only)*

- **attach / add-on** — pastry or second item per drink order; the ticket-lifting lever — *tell: drink-only visits, no food mention*
- **throughput / line speed** — orders served per hour at peak — *tell: "line out the door," slow morning rush*
- **regulars / daypart** — repeat customers; daypart = morning vs afternoon business mix — *tell: dead afternoons, churn language*

## Building a lexicon for a new sub-sector

1. Read what operators say to each other: trade forums and subreddits, trade-association benchmark reports, POS/software vendor glossaries. Collect failure modes, the levers operators cite, and recurring phrases.
2. **Curate down to metric/lever names.** Recurring phrases are mostly aphorisms ("busy doesn't always mean profitable") — those are not lexicon entries. Extract the *nouns operators manage by*: the KPIs, the levers, the named parts of the workflow.
3. For each term, write the **customer-visible tell** — the review pattern, benchmark, or count that lets you use the term without inventing a cause.
4. Drop anything you can't confirm is used correctly in that trade. Precision is the whole game; a wrong term is worse than plain English.
5. Add the entry here, dated. Prefer 6–10 tight metric/lever terms over an exhaustive glossary.
